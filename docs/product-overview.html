Red Team Operating Model

Turning an intake request into objective-based, independently verified red team deliverables, with AI doing the tedious work and humans keeping command. It follows a threat-informed, intelligence-led red team methodology: a capability-building and control-validation function, not an independent assurance function.

The problem

Intelligence-led red teaming drowns in intelligence. Threat reports, advisories, and framework updates arrive faster than anyone can turn them into a scoped, defensible engagement. The manual path is slow and inconsistent, and a single wrong technique mapping can send an engagement down the wrong road or undermine a stakeholder-facing deliverable. Engagements are objective-based, so the harder discipline is keeping every step tied to a business outcome with a measurable success criterion, not chasing flags or techniques. The goal is to compress intake and intelligence into action without giving up human judgment or leaking sensitive engagement data.

The operating model in one line

scope-> intel-> plan-> execute-> report-> handoff

Six phase skills each do one phase of the lifecycle and hand off over one shared engagement artifact, beginning at intake. You invoke them as slash commands (/rt-govern, /rt-intel, ...). Two authorization gates govern the run: Gate 1 signs off the Scoping Document (what is tested) and Gate 2 signs off the Rules of Engagement (how it runs, and authorizes execution). Both are signed by the Stakeholder, the Operations Lead, the Assessment Lead, and the CISO. An independent reviewer gates each producing phase fail-closed, and humans authorize execution and release. The skills are markdown instructions; the bundled code is small stdlib helpers (technique- and control-ID validation, optional machine formats, document conversion), with pandoc (docx) and pdfplumber (pdf) as external prerequisites for document conversion.

A standalone capability: vulnerability exposure

Aside from a full engagement, the team is often asked to act as subject-matter expert on a newly disclosed vulnerability. /rt-exposure (alias rt-vuln) does this on its own, with no governance gate and no engagement artifact: it dissects the vulnerability from a supplied advisory or CVE (the weakness, the affected surface, the exploitation path mapped to ATT&CK/ATLAS/AADAPT, and severity signals, each grounded in a verbatim quote), judges whether the organization is exposed, and proposes compensating controls. Each control is mapped to a NIST CSF Category and/or a NIST SP 800-53 control, with an explicit statement of how it limits the exposure and what residual risk remains. Two independent verifiers, one for the dissection and one for the controls, gate the assessment fail-closed and can run on a different model than the producer for uncorrelated errors.

Why composable, not a monolith

One big skill cannot check its own work honestly, cannot apply the right scrutiny per phase, and cannot cleanly separate what may leave the machine from what may not. Splitting the work into small skills makes verification independent, keeps each piece auditable, and lets the same authorization gates sit in front of execution and release. This mirrors how mature engineering-assistant systems are built: many focused skills over one shared artifact, with review isolated from authoring.

Framework coverage and trust

Offensive technique mappings are validated against three MITRE frameworks, so nothing invented slips through: ATT&CK Enterprise and ATLAS (AI/ML systems) fetched live, and AADAPT (digital-asset / payment tech) vendored. The reporting and compensating-control side (rt-report and rt-exposure) validates control IDs the same way against NIST CSF and NIST SP 800-53 (both vendored, public domain). OWASP is a sanctioned vocabulary for naming a web vulnerability class, but an OWASP-driven scope is a penetration test, redirected to that function, not this program's work. The split that makes verification trustworthy: the agent reads and judges; deterministic code checks everything checkable. Wrong or invented IDs are caught by code, not trusted to the model.

Proven end to end
A worked engagement runs govern -> intel -> emulate -> adapt -> report -> handoff, past two authorization gates and independent verification, on a crypto-custody scenario spanning ATT&CK and AADAPT.
Auditable by design
Fail-closed gating, two signed authorization gates, non-attribution in findings, and per-run catalog provenance (which data versions were used).

The validated outputs

Every engagement produces one auditable artifact whose sections are scoping, threat-profile, planning, execution, report, handoff, and verification, plus the human deliverables: the Engagement Report and the Executive Summary, with the Blue Team Account retained separately. Each deliverable carries only independently verified content; anything gated is flagged, not presented as final. Machine formats (JSON, STIX 2.1, ATT&CK Navigator layers) are optional and additive, produced only when a downstream consumer asks.

Honest limitations

  • With a hosted model, the reasoning steps send source content to the model provider. Full no-egress requires a local model or an enforced boundary. The tooling states this rather than overclaiming.
  • Live validation depends on reaching MITRE; an outage or air-gap gates the run (fail-closed) rather than passing unverified data. AADAPT and the NIST control catalogs are vendored and need periodic re-sync.
  • Verification independence is procedural, not organizational: the guarantee is a separate drafter, a fresh verifying context that never saw the producer's reasoning, and a report the tested function cannot edit (its own account is captured separately as the Blue Team Account).
